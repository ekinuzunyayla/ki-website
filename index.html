From wall push-ups to your first push-up

Ki is a step-by-step calisthenics plan for adults starting from zero. Short home workouts, about three times a week, with no gym equipment.

How it works

Four paths, each a ladder of small steps. You start where you are and move up when you’re ready.

Paths

One small step at a time

Four paths: Push, Pull, Lower Body and Core. Each path is a ladder of small steps. On Push that means wall push-ups, counter push-ups, stair push-ups, then floor push-ups.

A few quick questions and your first session set your starting step.

Equipment

No gym equipment

You use things you already have at home: a wall, a sturdy chair against the wall, the stairs if you have them, a backpack and a few water bottles.

Dumbbells are optional, and dumbbell mode is free.

The player

Log what you actually did

Tap the reps you did. Nothing is ever filled in for you.

Two moves share the same spot: one rests while the other works. Voice cues tell you what’s next, so you can follow along without watching the screen.

Safety, always one tap away

Make it easier, any set

“Make it easier” is on screen during every set. Pick an easier version, or a pain-free alternative if something hurts. Your progress is kept.

Stop if you feel sharp pain. If it continues, check with a professional.

A plan that fits real life

No daily streaks, no leagues, no guilt. Reminders only on the days you pick.

Voice cues, so you can look away

Short sessions with no jumping. Shorten any workout when time is tight.

A weekly streak that fits real life

Pick 2, 3 or 4 sessions a week. Rest days never break your streak, and a free streak freeze covers a week you miss.

Move up when you’re ready

Hit your target two sessions in a row to move up. Steps you’ve earned are never taken away.

Watch your reps add up

History and CSV export are included. Advanced charts come with Ki Pro.

What you’ll do

Ki is for you if you’ve never trained, haven’t trained in years, or just don’t want a gym. There’s no jumping, so it works in an apartment.

Push

Wall push-ups, counter push-ups, stair push-ups, then floor push-ups.

Pull

One-arm rows with a backpack of water bottles, or with dumbbells.

Lower Body

Sit-to-stands, chair squats, split squats and glute bridges.

Core

Dead bugs, planks and curl-ups.

Good to know

Ki is not medical advice. If you have a known heart, metabolic or kidney condition, or symptoms like chest pain, dizziness or unexplained shortness of breath, talk to a doctor before you start. Ki isn’t designed for pregnancy or the early postpartum period.
